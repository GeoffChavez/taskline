Reading the chart

The clear runway

1 September – 15 October

Six weeks with no graded technical submission due. The only stretch of the year like it. Everything that needs no vehicle, no pack and no test data should be finished inside it.

The pile-up

15 October – 19 November

Counting MarCom, PM and PIT, something is due almost every week: 1, 8, 15, 22, 29 Oct, then 5, 12, 19 Nov. Check every one for owner collisions.

The quiet risk

Spring — the two biggest

Architecture Selection (1 Apr, X-Large) and Baseline Vehicle Testing (6 May) are the largest deliverables of the year and the furthest from anyone’s attention today.

The external clock

Sponsored cell specifications

The 19 November battery workbook cannot start without them and their ship date is unknown. The dependency most likely to move a date we do not control.

Critical path — three chains

Everything on the chart resolves into three dependency chains. Where they cross is where the schedule actually breaks.

ChainSequenceWhat breaks it
Energy
most fragile		 Model proficiency → power demand analysis → per-cell C-rate check → pack configuration matrix → battery design section ×3 → down-select Sponsored cell specs arriving late. The C-rate answer then lands after the architectures are drawn, which is backwards.
Capability Off-road benchmarking → our targets → VTS values → three architecture definitions → integration assessment ×3 → selection The 29 October targets feed the VTS template directly. A weak targets report weakens every architecture argument that cites it.
Evidence Virtual model → EPA cycles (10 Dec) → baseline testing (spring) → model-vs-measured → architecture modeling ×3 Losing baseline testing does not stop the package — it strips the evidence reviewers probe hardest.

Suspension analysis: hand calculations first, then MATLAB and Simulink

We are adding up to 182 kg to a stock Jeep Cherokee, most of it at the rear. This guide shows how that mass changes the stock suspension, how to work out each effect by hand, and how to build models that check the hand calculations.

Why this matters, and what is in scope

The competition rules list different springs and dampers, including active suspension as permitted. That means it is allowed but probably not scored, and it may carry extra rules. So we are not redesigning the suspension.

What we do have to answer is what our added mass does to the stock suspension. The pack, rear drive unit, mounts, wiring and cooling can total 182 kg, much of it behind the rear axle line. On the same springs, that mass:

  • Lowers the rear. Ground clearance and departure angle feed our off-road targets.
  • Lowers the ride frequency and damping ratio. The car gets floatier and less controlled at the rear.
  • Moves load onto the rear axle. That sets how much torque our rear drive unit can put down.
  • Changes the halfshaft angles. Our new halfshafts have to stay inside the CV joint limits through full travel.
The working ruleDo every calculation by hand once, then check it in MATLAB. A model is trusted only when it agrees with a hand calculation, or the difference is explained. Report the difference as a percent error.

The eight hand calculations, in order

Each one uses results from the ones before it. Do them on paper first, with units on every line.

1

Corner weights and CG shift

ST-18
Wr = m·g·(1 − wf) rear axle load, stock xcg = (1 − wf)·L CG behind the front axle xcg,new = (m·xcg + Σ mixi) / (m + Σ mi) Wr,new = (m + Σ mi)·g·xcg,new / L
Tells you
How much extra load each rear corner carries, and whether the total stays inside 182 kg. Subtract anything removed, such as the stock rear driveline.
Example: 2,000 kg at 57/43 with 182 kg added near the rear axle. Rear axle load goes from 8,437 N to 10,140 N, and the rear share from 43.0% to 47.4%.
2

Motion ratio, wheel rate and ride rate

ST-19
MR = spring travel / wheel travel measure both on a jack Kw = ks · MR² wheel rate Kr = Kw·kt / (Kw + kt) ride rate: spring and tire in series
Watch out
MR is squared because the spring both moves less and pushes through a lever. Some books define MR the other way up. We always use spring travel over wheel travel.
Example: 50 N/mm spring, MR 0.85, tire 250 N/mm. Wheel rate 36.1 N/mm, ride rate 31.6 N/mm.
3

Static ride-height drop and ground clearance

ST-19
Δz = ΔWcorner / Kr ΔWcorner = (Wr,new − Wr)/2
Tells you
How many millimetres the rear sits lower. Subtract it from the rear ground clearance, then redo the departure angle with the lower tail.
Example: 852 N more per corner on 31.6 N/mm gives a 27 mm drop.
4

Ride frequency and damping ratio

ST-20
ms = Wcorner/g − mu sprung mass on one corner f = (1/2π)·√(Kr / ms) ride frequency, Hz cw = cd·MRd² damping at the wheel ζ = cw / (2·√(Kr·ms)) damping ratio
Tells you
Same springs with more mass means a lower frequency and less damping. Passenger SUVs usually sit somewhere around 1 to 1.5 Hz at the rear, so compare stock with converted and say what changed. Do not judge it against a single magic number.
Example: rear ride frequency 1.45 to 1.31 Hz; damping ratio 0.37 to 0.33.
5

Load transfer

ST-21
ΔWlong = m·ax·h / L front to rear under acceleration ΔWlat = m·ay·h / t inside to outside in a turn (whole car)
Tells you
How the corner loads move when the car launches, brakes or turns. Splitting the lateral transfer between front and rear needs roll stiffness (calculation 7).
Example: 0.4 g launch moves 2,088 N to the rear; 0.6 g cornering moves 5,516 N to the outside.
6

Rear-axle traction limit

ST-21
amax/g = μ·(Wr/W) / (1 − μ·h/L) rear-drive only, at the slip limit Tmax = μ·(Wr + m·amax·h/L)·rloaded
Tells you
The most torque the rear tires can use before they spin. Compare it with the rear drive unit's torque at the axle. Any torque above this limit is wasted, so it informs the control strategy.
Example: μ 0.8 gives 0.47 g with the rear axle alone, which is 3,326 N·m at the rear axle.
7

Roll gradient (stretch goal)

ST-21
φ/ay = ms·g·hra / (Kφf + Kφr − ms·g·hra) deg/g after unit conversion Kφ = ½·Kw·t² + Kbar per axle, N·m/rad
Needs
Anti-roll bar rates and roll-centre heights (hra is the CG height above the roll axis). Leave it as a placeholder until those are sourced.
8

Halfshaft angle through travel

ST-22
θ(z) = atan( (z0 − Δz + z) / Lshaft ) z from full droop to full bump
Tells you
Whether the CV joints stay inside their angle limit at full droop and full bump, from the new, lower ride height. Plot the angle against travel, with the joint limit as a line.
Example: a 0.55 m shaft over ±100 mm of travel runs from −11° at full droop to +9.6° at full bump. The 27 mm drop makes droop the worst case. That is inside an example ±20° limit.

Checking the hand calcs in MATLAB

The files are in the repository under matlab/suspension/. They run in MATLAB with no toolboxes, and they also run in the free GNU Octave.

cd matlab/suspension susp_handcalcs('example') % all eight calcs, teaching numbers quarter_car_ode('example') % quarter-car model and percent error vs calc 4 susp_handcalcs('cherokee') % stops and lists what ST-17 still has to measure
  1. Do the calculation by hand.
  2. Put your inputs in susp_params.m, with a source in the comment.
  3. Run susp_handcalcs and compare. If they disagree, find out which one is wrong before going on.
What the model teaches youOn the example set, the quarter-car body frequency lands within about 2% of hand calc 4, but the damping ratio comes out about 23% lower. The hand calc treats the wheel as fixed under the body. In the model the wheel moves, so the damper strokes less than the body does. Explaining a difference like that is the skill an employer is looking for.

Building it in Simulink: the quarter-car model

The two equations

ms·z̈s = −Fs mu·z̈u = Fs − Ft Fs = Kw(zs−zu) + cw(żs−żu) Ft = kt(zu−zr)

Measured from where the car sits at rest, so gravity drops out. Each acceleration goes through two Integrator blocks to give velocity, then position.

Build it by hand once

  1. Open a blank model. Set the solver to ode45, the stop time to 4 s and the max step to 1e-3.
  2. In the Command Window, define ms, mu, Kw, cw, kt and zr_step. Block dialogs hold names, not numbers, the same rule as the vehicle model.
  3. Body row: a Sum block for Fs, a Gain of -1/ms, then two Integrators (żs, then zs).
  4. Wheel row: a Sum block for Fs − Ft, a Gain of 1/mu, then two Integrators.
  5. Feed back: Sum blocks make zs−zu and żs−żu. Gains of Kw and cw turn them into Fs. A Step block is the road, and a Gain of kt on zu−zr gives Ft.
  6. Log zs and zu with To Workspace blocks, run the model, and plot.
  7. Read the body frequency and the decay off the plot, then compare with hand calc 4 and with quarter_car_ode.

Then run build_quarter_car_slx('example'), which builds the same model from a script and overlays it on the ode45 answer. The two should sit on top of each other.

Next model: half-car bounce and pitch

The quarter-car cannot show the body pitching. A half-car model uses the whole side of the car (front corner, rear corner and the body between them). It has two body states, bounce z and pitch θ, plus two wheel states.

m·z̈ = −Ff − Fr Iyy·θ̈ = a·Ff − b·Fr + Fx·h Fx·h = launch or braking moment zf = z − a·θ, zr = z + b·θ a, b = CG to front, rear axle

Drive it with a launch force at the rear axle (our rear drive unit) and plot pitch angle and both axle loads. Check: once the response settles, the axle loads must match hand calc 5. That's task ST-24. You also need a pitch inertia Iyy, which is [confirm]: estimate it and state how. After that, the Vehicle Dynamics Blockset and Simscape Multibody are the routes to full suspension geometry.

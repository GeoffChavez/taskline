The big picture

Five boxes, one job: push the tires.

Energy flows left to right. Each box changes what kind of energy it is.

1 · Battery

Stores energy as DC. Sets the voltage and how much current you can pull.

2 · Inverter

Turns DC into three-phase AC. Decides how much current reaches the motor, thousands of times a second.

3 · Motor

Turns AC into torque and speed. Power = torque × speed.

4 · Gearbox

Trades speed for torque: :1 means × torque, ÷ speed.

5 · Axle + tires

Half shafts carry torque to the wheels. Torque ÷ tire radius = push on the road.

On our drive unit, boxes 2–4 are one assembly (the inverter is the PIM bolted on top). The team designs box 1.

Step 1 · The battery

Series adds volts. Parallel adds amps.

A pack is a grid of identical cells. Name it by its shape: S cells in series, P in parallel, written like “96s2p”.

This is 3s2p: three in a row add voltage, two rows side by side add capacity.

Vpack = S × VcellVoltage adds along a string.
Ahpack = P × AhcellParallel strings share the current.
Energy = S × P × WhcellEvery cell contributes its watt-hours.
Mass = S × P × mcellCells only. Add the enclosure, busbars, BMS and cooling later.

Step 1 · The battery

Power is volts times amps. Small packs need high C-rates.

A cell’s C-rate says how hard it can be pushed: 10C on a 2 Ah cell is 20 A. Small pack, big motor, means every cell works hard.

P = V × I
Ipack = P × C × Ahcell
C needed = Pmotor ÷ EpackPower-to-energy ratio. 1 kW from a 1 kWh pack is 1C.

Step 1 · The battery

Pull hard and the voltage sags.

Every cell has internal resistance. Current through it costs voltage and makes heat. Sag can drop the pack below what the motor accepts.

Rpack = S × Rcell ÷ P
Vloaded = Vnom − I × Rpack
Heat = I² × Rpack
Imax above the motor floor = (Vnom − Vmotor,min) ÷ Rpack

Worked:

  • Rpack = × mΩ ÷ = mΩ
  • At full continuous current ( A): V, heat kW
  • Current limit to stay ≥ V: A
  • Usable power: kW continuous, kW peak

Datasheet resistance is measured at 1 kHz AC, which reads lower than real DC resistance. Real sag will be worse. These numbers are at nominal voltage; an emptier pack sags from a lower start.

Step 2 · The inverter

DC in, three-phase AC out.

The inverter switches the DC on and off very fast (PWM) across three phases, building AC at whatever frequency and strength the motor needs.

PDC = Pshaft ÷ ηη = inverter × motor efficiency. Get it from the efficiency map, not a guess.

Step 3 · The motor

Flat torque, then flat power.

Up to base speed the motor gives full torque. Above it, power stays flat and torque falls. The meeting point is the corner.

P = T × ω    ω = 2π × rpm ÷ 60
Corner speed = Ppeak ÷ Tpeak

Worked:

  • Peak axle torque N·m ÷ = N·m at the motor
  • At base speed rpm: kW
  • Corner for kW: motor rpm

The catch

Everything has to fit in one weight allowance.

The rules allow kg on top of the stock vehicle for the pack, enclosure, drive unit, mounts, wiring, cooling and instruments.

Worked:

  • Allowance kg − drive unit kg − cells kg = kg left
  • That remainder covers the enclosure, busbars, BMS, contactors, fuses, cooling, mounts and wiring.

Parts removed from the stock car (like the rear differential) may give mass back. Confirm the credit with the Vehicle Track lead before relying on it.
